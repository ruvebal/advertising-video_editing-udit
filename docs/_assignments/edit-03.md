# Edit 03: Self-Portrait — Final Project

This is your **final project** for the course: a **self-portrait in video form**. You are not selling a product or casting actors. You are representing **your inner world**—your influences, inspiration, your activity, and your work—through editing. The piece should feel like a portrait of _you_: how you see, think, and create.

You have **five weeks** to complete it. We will work **week by week**, in clear phases, with a strong focus on **video editing**. Pre-production and production are kept **light** on purpose: you have been incubating this idea; your job is to **gather materials as soon as possible** and **build the script**, so that most of your time goes into **post-production**—cutting, layering, and mixing.

The objective is to create **a piece that represents you and that you feel proud of**. A piece you can use in your **portfolio** or as its cover, and as a presentation across social media, platforms, and similar contexts.

## The Pitch — Applying Course Concepts (Weeks 1–10) {#edit-03-pitch}

Your **pitch** is a short document (about one page) that makes your project **real and executable**. It is not only a list of course concepts: it is a clear summary of your **intentions**, your **narrative strategy**, the **materials** you will use, and how you will design the experience across the **three platform versions**.

### Pitch checklist — what to include (Week 1)

Include these items in your pitch (bullet points are fine). Aim for clarity, not length.

- **Title + logline (1–2 sentences)**: What is this self-portrait *about*, in plain language?
- **Intentions (your “why”)**: What do you want the viewer to feel, understand, or remember about you?
- **Persuasion vs. convincing (Week 1 strategy)**:
  - Are you using **persuasion** (associative, emotional, atmospheric) or **convincing** (logical, structured, evidence/argument)?
  - What is your main rhetorical tool: contrast, repetition, escalation, intimacy, surprise, irony, confession, etc.?
- **Narrative strategy (shape + climax)**:
  - What is the structure? (e.g. montage poem, three acts, problem→shift→resolution, question→search→answer, before/after, spiral, diary, letter, manifesto.)
  - What is the **climax / turning point** (the moment the meaning crystallizes), and where does it happen roughly (timecode estimate)?
  - What is the ending gesture (resolution, open question, loop, call-back)?
- **Platform strategy (three versions)**:
  - What changes across **YouTube (16:9)** vs **Instagram (9:16/1:1)** vs **TikTok (9:16)**?
  - Where is the **hook** in each version (first 2–5 seconds), and how will you maintain clarity with captions/text where needed?
- **Materials inventory (initial list)**:
  - What do you already have (photos, archives, screen recordings, previous work, text fragments)?
  - What will you create or capture in the next 1–2 weeks (shots, voice-over, ambience recordings, graphics)?
  - What external resources will you use (music, SFX, stock) and how will you credit them?
- **Sound plan (four layers, mandatory)**:
  - What will be your **voice** (script idea, tone, language, distance/intimacy)?
  - What kind of **music** (tempo, mood) and how will it shape rhythm?
  - What kinds of **SFX** will you design (punctuation, transitions, textures)?
  - What **ambience** will define your “world” (room tone, city, nature, studio)?
- **Editing plan (workflow)**:
  - What is your plan for Week 3 (rough cut) and Week 4 (fine cut + mix)?
  - Which parts do you expect to be the hardest (clarity, pacing, voice recording, sound mix, reframing to vertical), and how will you handle them?

### Pitch checklist — course concepts to integrate (Weeks 1–10)

Use this as a guide when writing your pitch. You do not need a paragraph per item; you can group them, but each area below should be **reflected** in your concept and later in your edit.

**Week 1 – Persuasion & the medium**

- **McLuhan:** How does the _form_ of your piece (length, rhythm, platform) carry meaning?
- **Platform:** Who is the audience, and how does that shape your choices (e.g. vertical vs horizontal, captions)?
- **Ethics:** How are you being persuasive or expressive without being manipulative?

**Week 2 – Origins of cinema**

- **Lumière vs Méliès:** Will you use “reality” (actuality, documentary feel) and/or “trick” (in-camera magic, substitution, illusion)? How?
- **In-camera vs post:** What do you capture in camera vs create in the edit?

**Week 3 – Continuity & perception**

- **Continuity:** Where will you use continuity editing (180° rule, match on action, eyeline) to create coherence?
- **Gestalt:** How will you use closure, continuity, or proximity so the viewer “completes” meaning?
- **J-cuts / L-cuts:** How will sound lead or follow image at key moments?

**Week 4 – Montage**

- **Kuleshov:** Where will you use juxtaposition so that two shots create a third meaning?
- **Montage types:** Will you use metric, rhythmic, tonal, or intellectual montage? Where?
- **Rhythm:** How does cutting rhythm support the emotional arc?

**Week 5 – Breaking the rules**

- **Rule-breaking:** Will you use jump cuts, nonlinear time, glitch, or other experimental techniques? With what _purpose_?
- **Clarity vs chaos:** How do you keep the portrait readable while taking risks?

**Week 6 – Advertising & storytelling**

- **Structure:** Even as a self-portrait, does your piece have a clear shape (e.g. hook, development, resolution)?
- **Time constraint:** What duration have you chosen, and why? How does every second earn its place?

**Week 7 – Production**

- **Coverage:** If you shoot, what coverage (wide, medium, close-up, cutaways) do you need for the edit?
- **Team / solo:** Clarify how you’re working (solo or with minimal help).

**Week 8 – Post-production**

- **Color:** How will color correction/grading support mood and consistency?
- **Sound design:** How will you use the **four layers** (see below) to build space and emotion?
- **Polish:** What level of finish (titles, graphics, mix) are you aiming for?

**Week 9 – Platforms & ethics**

- **Platform:** You will deliver **three versions** (YouTube, Instagram, TikTok). How does each platform affect aspect ratio, length, captions, and hook? Plan for this in your edit.
- **Ethics:** How does your self-portrait relate to the attention economy, representation, or truth?

**Week 10 – Final showcase**

- **Presentation:** How will you introduce and contextualize this piece when you present it?

**Deliverable:** One-page pitch (or equivalent) that covers both sections above: (1) **intentions + strategy + materials**, and (2) **course concept integration**. Submit by the end of **Week 1** of the project (see timeline).

## Sound Design — The Four Layers (Mandatory)

Your self-portrait **must** use all **four layers of sound** as developed in the course (Weeks 8 and 11). No exceptions.

| **Layer**                    | **Role**                             | **Requirement**                                                                                        |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **1. Voice-over / dialogue** | Message, guidance, presence          | Must be present (narration, spoken word, or dialogue). Clearest element in the mix.                    |
| **2. Music**                 | Mood, emotion, rhythm of the edit    | Must be present. Supports, does not overpower. Duck under voice when both play.                        |
| **3. Sound effects (SFX)**   | Impact, realism, rhythm, punctuation | Must be present (e.g. whooshes, clicks, impacts, environmental sounds that punctuate cuts or moments). |
| **4. Ambience**              | Space, world-building, continuity    | Must be present (room tone, atmosphere, location sound—so the world feels real, not a vacuum).         |

**Mix hierarchy (reminder):**

1. Voice (clearest).
2. Important SFX.
3. Music.
4. Ambience (bed).

**Approximate levels (for reference):** Voice ~ -12 dB; music under voice (e.g. -24 dB when ducked); SFX contextual (~ -20 dB); ambience as bed (~ -24 to -18 dB). Use the Essential Sound panel (and Audition if available) to achieve a clear, professional balance.

In your **script** and **storyboard**, indicate _what_ appears in each layer for every section or scene.

## Materials — Image and Motion

Use **different kinds of materials** so that the edit feels rich and personal:

- **Text:** Titles, captions, quotes, on-screen type.
- **Motion graphics:** Simple animations, lower thirds, graphic elements (e.g. After Effects or Premiere).
- **Camera footage:** Anything you shoot—phone, camera, screen recording.
- **Stills / photographs:** Scanned images, phone photos, archives.

Every material is welcome. The mix of sources is part of the portrait. Plan in your script and storyboard _which_ type of material appears _where_.

## Three-Column Script

Before you lock the edit, you must produce a **three-column script** that describes the piece **section by section** (or shot by shot, if you prefer).

| **Column 1 — Visual**                          | **Column 2 — Audio**                      | **Column 3 — Technical / notes**                     |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| What we see: action, image, text, type of shot | What we hear: voice, music, SFX, ambience | Duration, transition, effect, layer notes, edit idea |

**Example:**

| **Visual**                                            | **Audio**                                                                      | **Technical notes**                     |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| Black. Fade up to close-up of hands typing.           | Ambience: room tone, keyboard clicks (SFX). Music fades in under.              | 5 s. Cut on first keystroke.            |
| Cut to photograph of old studio on desk.              | Voice-over begins: “I always worked in small spaces.” Music continues, ducked. | 4 s. J-cut: VO starts 0.5 s before cut. |
| Montage: three quick shots—window, plant, book spine. | VO continues. Music builds. SFX: page turn, distant traffic (ambience).        | 2 s each. Rhythmic montage to beat.     |

Produce the script **after** the mood board and **before** or alongside the first storyboard. It is the backbone of your edit.

## Storyboards (Two Passes)

You will create **two storyboards**, as in the Week 6 storyboarding annex.

**First storyboard**

- **Purpose:** First pass at the full piece—structure, beats, main shots.
- **Format:** Panels with: visual sketch or description, shot type, approximate duration, audio (dialogue/VO, music, SFX, ambience), notes.
- **Detail level:** Enough so that you (and a reader) understand sequence and pacing. Stick figures and rough sketches are fine.

**Second storyboard**

- **Purpose:** Refined version after re-reading your script and pitch. Fix clarity, timing, and coverage; ensure the four audio layers are planned per section.
- **Format:** Same as above, but cleaner and more precise. Add any new shots or cutaways you discovered you need.

**Storyboard panel reminder (from Week 6 annex):**

1. Visual (sketch or description).
2. Shot type (wide, medium, close-up, etc.).
3. Camera movement (static, pan, tilt, etc.).
4. Duration (seconds).
5. Audio (all four layers where applicable).
6. Notes (transitions, effects, edit ideas).

---

## Mood Board

Create a **mood board** (one page or one board) that captures:

- **Look:** Color palette, grain, contrast, references (films, ads, art).
- **Sound:** References for music style, texture, pace; types of SFX and ambience.
- **Feel:** Words, quotes, or images that sum up the _mood_ of your self-portrait.

This can be a PDF, a Figma/Canva board, or a simple collage. It will guide your script and storyboards and keep the edit consistent.

## Phases and Timeline (5 Weeks)

The process is divided into **pre-production**, **production**, and **post-production**. We keep pre-production and production **short** so that most of the five weeks is **editing**.

### Week 1 — Pre-production (pitch, mood board, materials)

**Goals:** Lock the idea, gather materials, create the script.

- **Pitch:** Write and submit your one-page pitch (concepts checklist, duration, materials, sound approach).
- **Mood board:** Create and submit your mood board.
- **Materials:** Start gathering _all_ materials: shoot what you need, collect photos, text, music, SFX, ambience. Organize in a clear folder structure (e.g. `01_RAW`, `02_ASSETS`, etc.).
- **Script:** Write the **three-column script** (Visual / Audio / Technical notes) for the whole piece.
- **Deliverables (end of Week 1):** Pitch, mood board, three-column script, list or confirmation of materials gathered.

### Week 2 — Pre-production (storyboards) + production

**Goals:** First storyboard, second storyboard, finish any remaining shooting or gathering.

- **First storyboard:** Complete the first full storyboard (all panels with visual, audio, duration, notes).
- **Production (minimal):** Shoot or record anything still missing. Keep it focused; the emphasis is on having _enough_ to edit, not on heavy production.
- **Second storyboard:** Refine into the second storyboard; double-check four-layer sound per section and clear pacing.
- **Deliverables (end of Week 2):** First and second storyboards, all materials ready for the edit.

### Week 3 — Post-production (rough cut)

**Goals:** Build the full rough cut in the timeline.

- **Edit:** Assemble the piece according to your script and storyboards. Use mixed materials (text, motion graphics, camera, stills).
- **Sound:** Lay in all four layers (voice, music, SFX, ambience) even if levels are rough.
- **Structure:** Ensure clear shape (hook, development, end) and that course concepts (continuity, montage, rule-breaking, etc.) appear where you planned.
- **Deliverable (end of Week 3):** Rough cut (full length, all layers present). Optional: share for peer or instructor feedback.

### Week 4 — Post-production (fine cut + mix)

**Goals:** Fine cut and proper sound mix.

- **Picture:** Fine cut—tighten pacing, refine transitions, fix continuity, add titles/graphics as planned.
- **Sound mix:** Balance the four layers (levels, ducking, EQ, compression). Voice clear; music and SFX and ambience in place. Use Essential Sound panel (and Audition if available).
- **Color:** Basic correction and grading for consistency and mood.
- **Deliverable (end of Week 4):** Fine cut with mixed audio and basic color. Ready for final export.

### Week 5 — Post-production (lock + export) + reflection

**Goals:** Lock picture and sound, export **three platform-specific versions**, write reflection.

- **Lock:** No more picture or sound changes unless critical.
- **Export:** Deliver **three versions** of your self-portrait, each optimized for a different platform (see **Output formats — three platform versions** below). You may need to reframe, trim, or adapt captions per platform.
- **Reflection:** Short written reflection (150–250 words) covering:
  - How your self-portrait represents your inner world, influences, and work.
  - Which course concepts you applied and where (give 2–3 concrete examples).
  - How you used the four sound layers and mixed materials.
  - How you adapted the piece for YouTube, Instagram, and TikTok (format, duration, captions).
  - What you would improve next time.
- **Deliverables (end of Week 5):** **Three final video files** (YouTube, Instagram, TikTok) + written reflection. Ready for final showcase (Week 10).

## Output formats — three platform versions

You must edit and export **three versions** of your self-portrait, one for each of the following platforms. Each version should be **optimized** for that platform (aspect ratio, duration, captions, hook). The same creative concept and four-layer sound design apply to all three; the **form** adapts.

| **Platform**  | **Aspect ratio** | **Duration**                                     | **What to optimize**                                               |
| ------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **YouTube**   | 16:9             | 6–60 s (or up to your chosen max; keep it tight) | Hook in first 5 s; skippable-audience mindset; sound-on.           |
| **Instagram** | 9:16 or 1:1      | 15–90 s                                          | **Captions essential** (many watch sound-off). Vertical or square. |
| **TikTok**    | 9:16             | 15–60 s                                          | **First 2 s critical**; loop-friendly if possible; vertical only.  |

**Practical notes:**

- You can master one “long” cut first, then create the three exports (reframe with Premiere’s auto-reframe or manual recompose; trim or restructure per platform).
- For Instagram and TikTok, ensure on-screen text/captions make the piece readable with sound off.
- Name each file clearly (see Technical requirements below).

---

## Technical Requirements

- **Exports:** H.264, 1080p minimum. Deliver **three files**:
  - **YouTube:** 16:9, e.g. `edit-03_LASTNAME_youtube.mp4`
  - **Instagram:** 9:16 or 1:1, e.g. `edit-03_LASTNAME_instagram.mp4`
  - **TikTok:** 9:16, e.g. `edit-03_LASTNAME_tiktok.mp4`
- **Project organization:** Use a clear folder structure (e.g. `01_RAW`, `02_SEQUENCES`, `03_EXPORTS`, `04_ASSETS`). Keep a separate sequence or export preset per platform if helpful. See the Resources page for the standard layout.

## Assessment Criteria (summary)

- **Pitch & concept:** Clear idea that addresses the course concepts (Weeks 1–10) and fits the self-portrait brief.
- **Script & storyboards:** Three-column script and two storyboards complete, coherent, and used in the edit.
- **Materials & form:** Effective use of mixed materials (text, motion graphics, camera, stills) and a clear structure.
- **Sound design:** **All four layers** (voice, music, SFX, ambience) present and mixed with clear hierarchy and appropriate levels.
- **Editing craft:** Application of continuity, montage, rhythm, and (where appropriate) rule-breaking with purpose.
- **Polish:** Coherent color, readable graphics, clean export.
- **Three platform versions:** All three exports (YouTube, Instagram, TikTok) delivered with correct aspect ratio, duration, and platform-appropriate optimization (e.g. captions for Instagram, strong hook for TikTok).
- **Reflection:** Thoughtful connection between the piece and course concepts, sound choices, materials, and adaptation across the three platforms.
