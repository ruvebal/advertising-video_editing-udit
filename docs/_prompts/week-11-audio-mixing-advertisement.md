# Week 11: Audio Mixing for Advertisement – Video, Cinema & Advertising

## Lesson Development Prompt for Instructors

<figure class="quote">
<blockquote>
Video is image plus sound. Treat both with the same care. The ear believes before the eye.
</blockquote>
<figcaption>
&mdash; Tao of the Video Editor
</figcaption>
</figure>

This week builds directly on **Week 8, Sound Design & Mixing**: it takes the four layers (dialogue, music, SFX, ambience), level targets, and Premiere Essential Sound panel as given and deepens them into a **full 2-hour session** on **audio mixing for advertising** using **Adobe Audition** and **Adobe Premiere Pro**. Students learn a professional round-trip workflow and apply it to a short ad mix.

**Software used:** Adobe Audition, Adobe Premiere Pro.

**Goal:** Students learn how **sound design and audio mixing improve advertising videos** and how to create a **basic professional audio workflow** using Audition and Premiere.

---

## Context & Learning Goals

### Departure from Week 8

Week 8 introduces the **four layers of sound**, level guidelines (-12 dB voice, ducked music, SFX, ambience), and the **Essential Sound panel** in Premiere. Week 11 assumes that foundation and adds:

- **Why** audio matters (demonstration and discussion)
- **Sound hierarchy** in advertising (voice first, then SFX, music, ambience)
- **Diegetic vs. non-diegetic vs. meta-diegetic sound** and how editors use them (sound bridge, J-cut, L-cut, diegetic music transition)
- **Round-trip workflow:** Premiere (edit) → Audition (clean and mix) → Premiere (export)
- **Audition-specific tasks:** cleaning dialogue, balancing levels, light compression, ducking
- **Analysis of famous ads** (Honda Cog, Guinness Surfer, Apple AirPods) for sound design
- **Hands-on exercise:** mix a 10–15 second ad with provided assets

### Learning Objectives

- Explain why audio carries emotion, rhythm, and brand identity in ads
- Name and order the four layers of sound and the sound hierarchy
- Define diegetic, non-diegetic, and meta-diegetic sound and give examples; describe sound bridge, J-cut, L-cut, and diegetic music transition
- Perform a Premiere → Audition → Premiere round-trip
- Clean dialogue (noise reduction, parametric EQ) in Audition
- Balance voice (~-12 dB), music (~-24 to -18 dB), and SFX (~-20 dB)
- Apply light compression to voice for consistency
- Use ducking so music lowers when dialogue plays
- Analyse famous advertisements for sound design choices
- Produce a short mixed ad (10–15 s) from provided materials

### Week 11 Success Indicators

By the end of Week 11, students should be able to:

- Demonstrate the same ad with and without sound and articulate what changed
- Describe the four layers and sound hierarchy
- Distinguish diegetic from non-diegetic sound in an ad or film example
- Export a sequence from Premiere to Audition and reimport the mixed audio
- Deliver a clean voice track and balanced mix with ducked music and one or two SFX
- Reference at least one famous ad when discussing sound design

## Session: Audio Mixing for Advertisement (2 hours)

### 0–10 min — Why Audio Matters

**Demonstration**

1. Show a short advertisement **with sound**.
2. Show the **same video muted**.
3. Ask the class: **What changed?**

**Discussion points**

- Sound carries **emotion**.
- Sound creates **rhythm**.
- Sound communicates **brand identity**.
- Sound helps the viewer **understand the message**.

**Key idea**

People tolerate bad image more than bad sound.

Introduce:

**Video = Image + Sound**

Both are equally important.

---

### 10–20 min — The Four Layers of Sound in Advertising

Explain that most advertisements are built using **four audio layers** (as in Week 8, with emphasis on advertising use).

#### 1. Voice-over

The message of the ad.

- Communicate information.
- Guide the viewer.

Voice should always be **the clearest element**.

#### 2. Music

Music defines:

- Mood
- Emotional tone
- Rhythm of the edit

Examples: fast music for an energetic brand; slow music for emotional storytelling.

#### 3. Sound effects (SFX)

SFX add:

- Impact
- Realism
- Rhythm

Examples in advertising: whooshes, clicks, impacts, product sounds. They often **highlight cuts or product shots**.

#### 4. Ambience

Ambient sound creates **space**: city noise, room tone, nature sounds. Even subtle ambience helps the video feel **more real**.

---

#### Sound hierarchy

Ask: **What should we hear first?**

Typical hierarchy:

1. Voice
2. Important SFX
3. Music
4. Ambience

---

### 20–25 min — Diegetic, Non-Diegetic, and Meta-Diegetic Sound

Introduce film-sound theory that editors and sound designers use to control **narrative meaning** and **emotional rhythm**. This distinction helps students choose what belongs "in the world" of the ad vs. "for the audience only."

#### Diegetic sound

**Definition:** Sound that exists **inside the story world** and is **perceptible to the characters**.

**Examples:**

- Dialogue between characters
- Footsteps on the floor
- A radio playing in a room
- A car engine
- A live band performing in a scene
- Ambient sounds (wind, traffic, crowd noise)

In editing and sound design this is sometimes called **source sound**.

#### Non-diegetic sound

**Definition:** Sound **external to the story world**, heard **only by the audience**, not by the characters.

**Examples:**

- Film score / background music
- Voice-over narration
- Dramatic sound effects added for emphasis
- Editorial musical montage

**Example:** In *Star Wars*, the orchestral score by John Williams is non-diegetic, because the characters do not hear it.

#### Hybrid: Meta-diegetic sound

Film theory also uses **meta-diegetic** for sounds that represent a **character's internal perception**.

**Examples:**

- Memory echoes
- Subjective ringing after an explosion
- Psychological sound distortions

Used frequently in films like *Apocalypse Now* or *Saving Private Ryan*.

#### Importance in video editing

Editors manipulate the **boundary between diegetic and non-diegetic sound** to control narrative meaning and emotional rhythm.

**Common techniques:**

1. **Sound bridge** — Diegetic sound continues across cuts to smooth transitions.
2. **J-cut** — Sound of the next scene (diegetic) begins **before** the visual cut.
3. **L-cut** — Sound from the previous scene continues **after** the visual cut.
4. **Diegetic music transition** — Music starts as diegetic (e.g. radio in the room) and becomes non-diegetic score. Example: A character turns on a radio; the song plays in the room; the same song expands into the full soundtrack over a montage.

#### Theoretical references

The distinction is widely discussed in:

- **Bordwell & Thompson,** *Film Art: An Introduction*
- **Michel Chion,** *Audio-Vision: Sound on Screen* (Chion also introduces concepts such as **acousmatic sound** — a sound whose source is unseen.)

#### Short definition (exam-style)

**Diegetic sound** is audio that originates **within the narrative world** of the film and **can be heard by the characters**, such as dialogue, environmental sounds, or music produced inside the scene.

### 25–35 min — Professional Audio Workflow

Explain the workflow used in many studios.

**Step 1.** Edit video in **Premiere**.

**Step 2.** Send the sequence (or mix stems) to **Audition**.

**Step 3.** Clean and mix audio in Audition (dialogue, music, SFX, ambience).

**Step 4.** Return the final mixed audio to Premiere.

**Step 5.** Export the final video from Premiere.

**Why this workflow:** Premiere for editing and picture; Audition for dedicated audio processing and mixing.

**Demonstrate live:** Export from Premiere to Audition (Edit → Edit in Adobe Audition → Sequence), then reimport the mixed file or use dynamic link if available.

---

### 35–60 min — Working in Adobe Audition

Open **Audition** and show a basic session. Focus on three tasks.

---

#### 1. Cleaning dialogue

Dialogue often contains:

- Background noise
- Hum
- Room resonance

**Useful tools in Audition:**

- Noise Reduction (capture noise print, then reduce)
- Parametric Equalizer (cut rumble, boost presence around 3 kHz)

**Goal:** Make the voice **clear and intelligible**.

---

#### 2. Balancing levels

Use level guidelines commonly used in advertising:

| Element    | Target level   |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Voice-over| about **-12 dB**|
| Music     | about **-24 to -18 dB** |
| Sound effects | about **-20 dB** |

**Goal:** Clarity, not loudness. Voice must always be easy to understand.

---

#### 3. Compression

Light compression helps voice stay **consistent**:

- Reduce loud peaks
- Make quiet parts clearer

Remind students: **Too much compression sounds unnatural.**

---

### 60–75 min — Music and Ducking

**Common problem in student work:** Music is too loud.

Introduce **ducking**: music automatically **lowers in volume when dialogue (or VO) starts**.

In Audition this can be done with:

- Automation (clip gain or track volume)
- Manual keyframes

This technique is **used constantly in advertising**.

**Demonstrate:** Draw or automate a dip in the music track under the voice.

---

### 75–90 min — Famous Advertising Examples (Sound Analysis)

Show three famous ads and analyse their sound design.

---

#### 1. Honda — "The Cog"

This commercial is built almost entirely around **sound effects**.

- Mechanical sounds create rhythm.
- No heavy music is needed.
- Tension is created through timing.

**Discussion:** How does the sound build anticipation?

Students often notice that **every sound acts like percussion**.

*Instructor: embed with video-player include when YouTube ID is confirmed; e.g. search "Honda The Cog commercial".*

---

#### 2. Guinness — "Surfer"

Famous for its **dramatic sound atmosphere**.

- Powerful music
- Ocean ambience
- Slow pacing

Sound builds tension, emotion, and brand identity.

**Discussion:** What emotion does the sound create?

Typical answers: epic, mythic, powerful.

*Instructor: embed with video-player include when YouTube ID is confirmed; e.g. search "Guinness Surfer advert".*

---

#### 3. Apple — "AirPods Bounce"

Shows how **music can drive editing**.

- Choreography synced with music
- Cuts aligned with beats
- Rhythmic motion

**Lesson:** Sound can define the visual rhythm of an edit.

**Question:** Would this ad work without music?

*Instructor: embed with video-player include when YouTube ID is confirmed; e.g. search "Apple AirPods Bounce ad".*

---

### 90–110 min — Student Exercise

Students create a **short audio mix for a 10–15 second ad**.

**Provide:**

- One video clip (picture only or with temp audio)
- Voice-over
- One music bed
- Two or three sound effects

---

#### Instructions

**Step 1.** Import or receive the audio assets. Open the video in Premiere; send to Audition or import stems into Audition.

**Step 2.** Clean the voice track (noise reduction, EQ).

**Step 3.** Balance voice and music (voice ~-12 dB, music ~-24 dB).

**Step 4.** Add one or two sound effects; set level ~-20 dB as starting point.

**Step 5.** Apply ducking so music lowers when the voice plays.

**Step 6.** Export the mix from Audition; import the final audio into Premiere and replace the temporary audio on the timeline.

**Step 7.** Export the final video from Premiere.

---

#### Recommended levels (reminder)

| Element | Level |
|---------|--------|
| Voice   | about **-12 dB** |
| Music   | about **-24 dB** (under voice: duck further if needed) |
| SFX     | about **-20 dB** |

**Goal:** Voice must always be easy to understand.

---

### 110–120 min — Review and Discussion

Play a few student results (with permission).

**Ask the class:**

- What worked well?
- What could be improved?

**Discussion prompts:**

- Was the voice clear?
- Was the music too loud?
- Did sound effects improve the edit?

**Takeaway:** Good audio makes an advertisement feel professional.

---

## Final Key Ideas for Students

Summarise these five points:

1. Advertising sound uses **layers** (voice, music, SFX, ambience).
2. **Diegetic** sound is in the story world (characters hear it); **non-diegetic** is for the audience only (score, VO). Editors use this boundary for meaning and rhythm (sound bridge, J-cut, L-cut).
3. Voice must always be **clear** and intelligible.
4. Music creates **emotion** and rhythm; duck it under voice.
5. Good audio mixing makes videos **feel professional**.
